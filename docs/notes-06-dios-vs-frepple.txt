

DIOS vs frePPLe

How Makkook DIOS works, and what completes frePPLe

Research, public sources only, for your frePPLe fork

Researched Nov 20, 2026, 12:15am

DIOS is a model compiler: it turns a client's data into one huge optimization model and solves it. frePPLe can't do that. You'd build the compiler yourself on Pyomo or OR-Tools.

No leak exists. The best public source is the founder's own Shark Tank Egypt pitch, aired Nov 18. I downloaded it and transcribed all 28 minutes myself.

Facts are marked Confirmed (with a link) or Inference (my reading). Every pitch number comes from my automatic Arabic transcript, so check the video at the timestamps given.

One giant model, compiled from templates.In the pitch the founder says ready-made modules (sector, then industry, then variant like make-to-order or make-to-stock) are composed into one maths model. His example has about 2 million constraints and 1.4 million decision variables, sent to a solver. Shark Tank, ~4:40-6:30 makkook.ai/dios

The optimisation brain is an academic metaheuristics team.The Chief Optimization Officer, Prof. Ali Khater, co-created the Gaining-Sharing Knowledge (GSK) algorithm and won IEEE CEC 2021. So DIOS probably mixes exact solvers with custom evolutionary search. about-us GSK paperMix is inference.

No LLM in the core, on purpose.The founder says LLMs are probabilistic and leave no audit trail, so DIOS is deterministic and explains the trade-offs behind each decision. Their own article describes 6.5 million decisions, 500,000 constraints and 9 objectives in one F&B plan. makkook.ai article

frePPLe CE covers about a third.It holds the data, the planning screens, scenarios and "why is this late". It can't do the model compiler, weighted objectives, minimising changeovers, replanning only what changed, routing or staffing. frePPLe source

Recommended: frePPLe as the system of record plus an optimisation service.Use Pyomo/linopy with HiGHS and CP-SAT for the compiler, Timefold and PyVRP for staffing and routing, Nixtla for forecasts, Debezium and Temporal for event-driven replanning. The compiler and the industry libraries have to be built in-house. No repo has them.

Part 1: how DIOS works

The pitch: architecture, in the founder's wordsConfirmed from the video, via my Whisper transcript (timestamps approximate).

Confirmed: industry modules are layered as sector, then industry, then variant (MTO/MTS). An entity model with relations between items, machines, workers and more is composed into one model of ~2M constraints and ~1.4M variables, then solved. ~4:40-6:30

Confirmed: the client's IT team gives API access to SAP, and Makkook maps it to its own database. WMS and TMS are named too. It offers alternatives with their trade-offs, and replans only the part a user is allowed to change.

Confirmed: 50+ operational decision families. He calls it deterministic and says it uses no LLM in the core. He names o9 and RELEX as competitors.

The relation types point to a Palantir-style ontologyInference from one phrase in the pitch.

The transcript has him describing relations as roughly "kinetic, semantic, transactional" (~5:30). Whisper may have misheard part of that.

Inference: "semantic" and "kinetic" are Palantir Foundry's words for objects and links versus actions. So DIOS likely keeps a typed graph of business objects that the compiler reads.

The DIOS page agrees: a pre-built model of "items, resources, workers, locations, time, demand, cost" that it "compiles" into an executable maths model. makkook.ai/dios

Recommended reference architecture
1. Data inPull ERP data and listen for changes.

dlt for batch loads from ERP, WMS and TMS. Debezium into Kafka for live changes. Node-RED, opcua-asyncio and pymodbus for machines.

Pandera checks every table before it reaches the model. Data lands in Postgres, plus TimescaleDB for sensor data.

2. frePPLe as the system of recordIts entities, screens, scenarios and "why late" stay as they are.

Your canonical entity graph maps onto frePPLe's item, resource, location, demand, setupmatrix and skill tables through its REST API.

frePPLe's own planner stays as a fast fallback and for its delay and problem reports.

3. Optimisation service (the DIOS-level part)A Python service next to frePPLe.

The in-house compiler reads the entity graph plus a decision template and builds Pyomo/linopy models. HiGHS or SCIP solves them, and CP-SAT or PyJobShop handles changeovers.

Timefold covers staffing, PyVRP with OSRM covers routing, and stockpyl covers stock targets. pymoo or weighted sums handle the objectives, and mealpy is the fallback when exact solves are too slow.

Forecasts come from Nixtla and feed frePPLe's forecast API, tracked in MLflow. Results write back as frePPLe orders.

4. Replanning, audit and screensEvents trigger re-solves; everything is logged.

A Kafka event starts a Temporal workflow, which re-solves the affected window with the rest fixed and puts the result up for approval.

django-pghistory or Dolt keeps every plan version and the three decision-latency timestamps. vis-timeline, Superset and apprise handle the Gantt, KPIs and alerts.

What no repo gives you (build in-house)This is where DIOS's value is.

The entity-to-model compiler and the 50+ industry decision templates, each tested on real client data.

Replanning only the affected window, explaining trade-offs between alternatives, and the screen for setting objective weights.

Per-client SAP adapters, and speed at the ~1.4M-variable scale. I didn't test open-source solvers at that size, so benchmark them on your data early.
